2. The Big Picture — How a Project Moves Through the System

Here's what we think the end-to-end process looks like, from the moment someone identifies work that needs to be done through project closeout:

Step by Step

StepWhat HappensWho Does ItDocuments Produced
1. Identify Work is identified at a property — could be planned (capital budget) or unplanned (emergency repair, inspection finding). Gets a description and urgency level. PM, Field Staff, or Customer Request Row added to Tracker
2. Scope & Estimate Figure out what trades are needed, what materials, roughly how much it will cost. For bigger jobs, a full estimate with takeoff measurements. PM + Estimator Estimate template (if needed)
3. Decide: Sub or Self-Perform Is Decades doing this work with their own crew, or sending it out to subs? This decision determines whether you go to bid or go straight to scheduling. PM / Leon —
4. Send RFP If subbing out: pick vendors by trade, send them the scope, ask for pricing. Might be one trade or multiple trades bundled. PM / Leon RFP package / bid invite
5. Collect & Compare Bids Vendors send back proposals (usually PDFs with pricing). Enter numbers into the bid comparison template. Compare apples to apples across vendors. PM Completed Bid Comp template
6. Award Pick the winning vendor (or split across vendors by trade). Document the recommendation and get sign-off. PM → Leon / Bill for approval Bid Req tab, Exhibit A, Exhibit B
7. Execute Work gets done. Track invoices, handle change orders, manage schedule, deal with surprises. PM + Subs Invoices, Change Orders, Submittals
8. Closeout Work complete. Punch list, final inspections, collect warranty letters and FROLs, capture final costs. PM Closeout package
Key insight from your tracker: Not every project goes through every step. A small single-trade repair might go straight from "Identify" to "Award" (you already know who does this work). A $200K multi-trade renovation goes through all 8 steps. The process scales based on the size and complexity of the job.

6. How Work Gets Scoped and Estimated

What We Think Happens

When a project is identified, the PM figures out what work needs to be done. For a small job, this might be as simple as "replace bathroom fixtures — call the plumber." For a larger job, it involves a full estimate with measurements (takeoffs), material lists, and cost calculations.

The Estimate Template (6 Tabs)

From the Tyler Drive budget workbook, we found a 6-tab estimate template:

TabWhat It Contains
Sage BudgetRolls up to the Sage cost code structure — this is what feeds the accounting system
BudgetDetailed budget breakdown: labor hours × rate + materials × price × tax = total per line item
BuysheetMaterial purchasing list — what needs to be bought, from where, at what price
TakeoffMeasurements from the field — square footage, counts, linear feet. The raw numbers that drive the budget.
Door TakeoffSeparate takeoff specifically for doors (common enough to warrant its own sheet)
Material SpentTracks actual material spend against the buysheet budget as the project progresses
Two cost models: The budget has two ways to calculate cost:
  • Labor: hours × hourly rate = labor cost
  • Materials: quantity × unit price × (1 + tax rate) = material cost
Total project cost = labor + materials + equipment + subcontractor + other.

  3. The Buysheet — is this used for self-perform work where Decades is buying materials? Or also for subcontracted work?
  4. For repeat work (e.g., you've done 30 bathroom rehabs) — do you still estimate each one, or do you have a standard cost per property that you just apply?

Step 1: Picking Vendors

When work needs to go out to bid, you look for vendors who:

  • Do the trade(s) you need (plumbing, electrical, etc.)
  • Are reasonably close to the property
  • Have an active MSA (Master Service Agreement) with Decades
  • Have done good work before (or are worth trying)

Step 2: Sending the RFP

You send vendors the scope of work and ask for pricing. This might be:

  • A single trade for one property ("bid plumbing at 310 Cranford")
  • A single trade across many properties ("bid bathroom rehabs at these 15 group homes")
  • Multiple trades for a complex project ("bid plumbing + electrical + HVAC for this renovation")

Step 3: Collecting Proposals

Vendors send back proposals — usually PDF documents with their pricing. From the Tyler Drive project, we saw proposals follow this naming pattern:

Naming format: {Job#} - {Address} - Proposal - {Vendor Name} - ${Amount}.pdf
Example: 22003 - 153 Tyler - Proposal - Active Environmental - $9882.pdf

Step 4: Entering Bids

Someone takes the numbers from the vendor PDF proposals and enters them into the bid comparison template (the 19-tab workbook — see Section 8).

Step 5: Comparing

The bid comp template has formulas that compare vendors side by side within each trade. The Dashboard tab rolls up the comparison. The Bid Req tab is where you write the recommendation and capture the award decision.

8. The Multi-Trade Bid Comparison Template (19 Tabs)

This is the centerpiece of the bid process. We analyzed the full structure:

Tab Layout

#Tab NamePurpose
1DashboardSummary view — rolls up all trade comparisons, shows totals, status
2Bid TrackerTracks which vendors have been invited, who responded, bid amounts, status
3Bid ReqThe award recommendation — which vendor was selected for each trade and why. This is the decision document.
4Exhibit BScope of work document that gets attached to the contract
5-1713 Trade TabsOne tab per trade — lists scope items as rows, vendors as columns (up to 5 bidders per trade at columns D, F, H, J, L). Each vendor's pricing entered side by side for apples-to-apples comparison.
18-19(varies)Additional reference or summary tabs

How a Trade Tab Works

Rows = scope items (line items of work — e.g., "Floor tile removal," "Tub/shower install," "Fixture rough-in")

Columns D, F, H, J, L = up to 5 different vendor bids

Each cell = that vendor's price for that scope item

Bottom row = total per vendor

Formulas use CHOOSE/MATCH/INDIRECT to pull the winning bid into the Bid Req tab automatically.

Cross-Sheet Logic

The template uses Excel formulas that connect tabs together:

  • Dashboard pulls totals from each trade tab
  • Bid Req pulls the selected vendor's pricing from each trade tab
  • Exhibit B pulls scope items and awarded pricing

Billing Constraints

From conversations with Joe, we understand Bancroft has two simultaneous limits on billing:

  1. Milestone cap: Can't bill past a milestone you haven't completed
